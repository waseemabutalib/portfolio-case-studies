# Omeka S Digital Humanities Archive

## Overview

I designed, developed, and deployed a public-facing digital humanities research archive using Omeka S and Reclaim Hosting. The project translated archival research into a searchable web experience with metadata workflows, document access, responsive layouts, and room for long-term dataset expansion.

This version is sanitized for public GitHub. Client names, raw datasets, full theme files, and private project materials are intentionally omitted unless separately approved for public sharing.

- UX Engineer
- CMS Developer
- Digital Humanities Technologist

## Context

The project needed a website and archive structure that could present research, organize records, support metadata, and remain maintainable as the dataset grows. The work required both technical implementation and careful UX/information architecture decisions so public users and future maintainers could navigate the archive clearly.

## My Role

- Designed and developed the website/archive infrastructure.
- Built the project in Omeka S.
- Deployed and supported the site through Reclaim Hosting.
- Coordinated directly with the client throughout the project lifecycle.
- Translated research needs into public-facing structure, UI, metadata workflows, and long-term management planning.

## What I Built

- Searchable archive system.
- Returned artifacts section.
- Document library.
- Metadata integration workflows.
- Public-facing page structure.
- Responsive layouts.
- UI/UX patterns for browsing and reading.
- Reclaim Hosting deployment.
- Long-term sustainability planning for future dataset expansion and site management.

## UX And Information Architecture

Key design needs included:

- Make the archive understandable to public users.
- Support research-driven browsing and search.
- Separate artifacts, documents, and contextual research material.
- Keep the structure expandable for future data.
- Make site management practical for the client after launch.

## Implementation Process

1. Understand the research goals and content model.
2. Identify core content types, archive sections, and metadata needs.
3. Configure Omeka S structure and public site organization.
4. Build responsive page layouts and navigation.
5. Integrate documents, records, and searchable archive behavior.
6. Review with the client and adjust based on feedback.
7. Deploy through Reclaim Hosting and plan for future maintenance.

## Accessibility And Usability

Accessibility and usability mattered because the archive needed to serve public users, researchers, and future maintainers. I focused on clear navigation, readable layouts, searchable/browsable archive structure, responsive behavior, and metadata organization that makes records easier to find and understand.

## Outcomes

- Delivered a live public-facing research archive.
- Created an expandable archive structure.
- Supported client ownership and future site growth.
- Translated complex research material into a navigable digital publishing experience.
- Strengthened practical experience with Omeka S, Reclaim Hosting, metadata workflows, UX, and client delivery.
